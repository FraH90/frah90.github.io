When a transmission line is terminated in a load impedance $Z_L \neq Z_0$, not all the incident
power is absorbed. The remainder reflects back toward the source, creating a **standing wave** pattern.

The model below is a lossless uniform line with positive real characteristic impedance and a passive load. The load is at $z = 0$ and the source at negative $z$; incident voltage amplitude is normalized to 1.

## Reflection Coefficient

The voltage reflection coefficient at the load:

$$
\Gamma_L = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}
$$

$|\Gamma_L|$ ranges from 0 (perfect match) to 1 (total reflection — a purely reactive load, short, or ideal open circuit).

The voltage along the line is the superposition of incident and reflected waves:

$$
V(z) = V^+\!\left(e^{-j\beta z} + \Gamma_L\, e^{+j\beta z}\right)
$$

## Voltage Standing Wave Ratio

$$
\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|}
$$

VSWR = 1 means perfect match. VSWR = ∞ means total reflection.

## Special cases

- **Matched** ($Z_L = Z_0$): $\Gamma = 0$, flat voltage profile, all power delivered.
- **Short circuit** ($Z_L = 0$): $\Gamma = -1$, voltage null at the load, current maximum.
- **Open circuit** ($Z_L \to \infty$): $\Gamma = +1$, voltage maximum at the load, current null.

For a mismatched lossless line, adjacent voltage-envelope minima are separated by $\lambda/2$. A matched line has a constant envelope and no distinct minima.

A short circuit has a voltage **minimum at the load** and a **maximum** $\lambda/4$ toward the source. An ideal shorted quarter-wave line presents an open input impedance. A quarter-wave impedance transformer instead uses a suitable characteristic impedance to match a specified load.
