The DFT assumes the $N$-point signal is one period of an infinitely periodic sequence.
When the signal frequency is not an exact integer multiple of $\Delta f = f_s / N$,
the periodic extension has a boundary discontinuity and the observed tone spreads across bins.
This is **spectral leakage**.

## Why It Happens

Selecting $N$ samples is equivalent to multiplying an infinite signal by a rectangular window.
In the frequency domain, multiplication becomes convolution with the window's spectrum — a periodic sinc-like (Dirichlet) response. Ignoring its linear phase, its magnitude is:

$$
|W_\text{rect}(f)| = \left|\frac{\sin(\pi f N / f_s)}{\sin(\pi f / f_s)}\right|
$$

At removable singularities, use the limiting value. The rectangular window has high sidelobes (first sidelobe at −13 dB). Any spectral component leaks into
adjacent bins through these sidelobes.

The spectrum shows one-sided peak amplitude, normalized by the sum of window coefficients. This corrects coherent gain; off-bin tones still suffer scalloping and nearby components can interfere. DC and Nyquist bins are not doubled. The default frequencies are deliberately off-bin.

## The window trade-off

Approximate large-N sidelobe levels and null-to-null widths (cycles/sample):

| Window | First sidelobe | Main lobe width |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular | −13 dB | $2/N$ |
| Hann | −32 dB | $4/N$ |
| Hamming | −43 dB | $4/N$ |
| Blackman | −58 dB | $6/N$ |

For these windows, lower distant sidelobes come with broader main lobes. Choose based on whether you care more about resolving closely-spaced frequencies (use rectangular or Hann) or detecting weak signals next to strong ones (use Blackman).

## Practical rule

For isolated bin-centered tones in a noiseless coherent record, a rectangular window can preserve narrow spectral lines without leakage into other DFT bins. For off-bin tones, a Hann window is a useful starting point, but the choice depends on dynamic range, resolution, amplitude accuracy, and noise. Correcting coherent gain does not remove scalloping loss.
