# Visual guidelines

These are the current design trials from the owner's 2026-11-02 feedback. Implementation follows these rules; final visual approval remains open in the primary roadmap. Change centralized dimensions in `src/styles/settings.css` rather than scattering new widths or colors through components.

## Proportions and navigation

Use a wider archive canvas (1216px maximum) for media and the grid, and the existing 880px article canvas for reading and applets. Consistency means shared spacing, hierarchy and aligned edges within each view, rather than forcing every page into one width. On roomy desktop screens, header identity/navigation align with the current canvas; outline/Explore controls remain at viewport edges. On smaller screens, reserve space for usable controls instead of forcing desktop alignment. Docked desktop panels reserve content space and the header follows that available space. Keep the slim header and established panel motion.

## Media is an article-owned slot

`ArticlePreview.astro` is the shared boundary for feature and grid media. An article can supply a typed image, GIF with still poster, or video. A custom interactive preview requires an explicit browser-safe renderer with a static alternative. These types are supported now; authors do not need to redesign cards when adding their own assets. See [media metadata](tags-and-previews.md).

Keep controls within the interactive preview frame. Use compact labels, thin tracks and small monospace readouts, consistent with the article demos. A preview can use a lighter engine or simpler interaction, but must retain readable axes/units, correct data, accessible context and valid boundary cases. Static card plots use larger axis names/units, three grid lines per axis and no miniature numeric ticks or repeated chart title. Keep the full numerical labels in article figures; the larger interactive feature retains its ticks. Keep full setup/provenance in accessible descriptions. Do not display redundant titles or both axis zero labels. Do not decimate data differently after the reader touches a control: the array preview now uses the same 721-point model before/after steering, with one-degree controls and frame-coalesced drawing. Do not smooth curves in a way that changes their numerical meaning.

Do not autoplay GIF/video or decorative motion. Keep a still/no-JavaScript alternative and playback controls independent of the article link. Source figures from the work, not invented measurements. Label calculated and illustrative material honestly. Keep heavyweight Plotly/React demo engines off the archive unless justified by a measured need. Article demos keep their established Plotly presentation and scientific models.

## Color roles

Global navigation, theme controls, prose, headings, equations, grid lines and axes stay neutral in both themes. Article identity comes from its primary category in `blog/taxonomy.json`, never from an arbitrary child tag. Article links, controls/readouts, closing takeaway border and the active Contents entry use that category accent. The category token is available on the page root so the Contents drawer retains it when moved into its dialog. Demo remains neutral gray. Related-card metadata and preview controls retain the related article’s own category identity.

Scientific color expresses a quantity or comparison. Use the category hue for the primary result where no scientific convention takes precedence; choose distinguishable secondary hues from the shared theme palette. Keep each role consistent across previews, static fallbacks and live plots through `src/lib/demo-colors.ts`. DSP uses fuchsia for both time-domain and spectrum panels; optics uses orange for both signed boundaries, with a dashed negative boundary. Different quantities or comparisons may still use distinct colors when they aid interpretation. Labels, sign conventions and line styles distinguish traces in addition to hue. Never force externally sourced figures, syntax highlighting, scientific conventions or every comparison curve into one category color. Do not recolor numerical data or imply a new scientific meaning.

Article text selection uses a subdued translucent category tint (34% in dark mode, 18% in light mode) with normal theme ink, echoing the historical deep-green selection. Do not blend with white or force dark selected text in dark mode. Global selection remains neutral. Keep palette values in `src/styles/settings.css`. The global accent is neutral; `BlogPostLayout` scopes its article accent without recoloring the header or Explore. Scientific renderers resolve their registered theme colors and redraw on explicit theme changes without resetting controls. Test text contrast, category/neutral separation and trace consistency in light/dark, live/fallback and mobile/desktop views.

## Category-owned refinements

Show one colored, outlined category badge beside the date. Tags and Demo must not form extra visible rows on articles, cards, the feature or search results. The badge is a native disclosure: mouse hover reveals its contents when scripts are available; click/tap and Enter/Space work with or without scripts. Show only the specific tags of this article registered under that category and its independent Demo status; do not add an All [category] articles row. Close when pointer hover and keyboard focus have left, or on Escape/outside interaction. Keep its width within the metadata row and preserve independently operable links.

Tags narrow a selected category, rather than acting as a global unrelated list. `blog/taxonomy.json` registers their parent category; `/categories/<category>/<tag>` shows published members of that category with the tag. In Explore, category text is an underlined navigation link, while the plus sign and unused row space are a separate native disclosure for that category’s specific tags. Keep the link and disclosure as siblings, not nested interactive controls. Do not show All articles or All [category] articles rows. Categories without specific tags need no empty expander. Existing `/tags/<slug>` links remain compatible, including legacy broad cross-subject associations.

Use the category hue for its badge; mix that hue toward theme ink for its tag pills, making them slightly lighter in dark mode without losing contrast in light mode. Demo is neutral gray status inside an article disclosure, with its transient explanation. It remains the canonical `demo` boolean, not a category-owned tag or selectable filter. Never put Demo under Explore categories. Previously published `/categories/<category>/demo` links redirect to the category and are excluded from the sitemap. Do not autoplay motion or show the Demo explanation persistently.

## Focus and interaction

Never draw a large accent-colored frame around an entire article card. Keyboard focus uses a title underline for cards, text underlines for disclosures and small internal underline/stroke markers for icon controls. Icon controls never draw outer focus rings, including native modal autofocus. Programmatically focused main/heading/live-demo wrappers never show a frame. Text links and demo text buttons use keyboard underlines; category expansion and plot-expand icons use local marks. Scrollable figures, code, tables and equations use an inset bottom line for keyboard focus. Inputs retain clear neutral focus outlines. Preserve native focus, keyboard activation and modal focus handling. Pointer/touch interaction must not leave keyboard-style frames behind; switching back to the keyboard restores visible cues. Keep adequate hit areas even when labels are visually small. Hover is an enhancement, not required navigation.

Demo explanations are transient hover/focus/tap tooltips. Keep them within the metadata row's available width so even hidden tooltip geometry cannot widen the page. Keep tag/category links and media controls independently operable; no nested links or repeated Read article button.

## Review before delivery

Check desktop/phone widths, both themes, keyboard/touch media interaction and focus, no-JavaScript navigation/static media, media failure, scientific labels and published/draft routes. Use actual browser measurements for alignment/overflow and view screenshots. Record verified behavior and any physical-device or owner-media limitations in the ledger. A successful implementation check does not replace the owner's final design review.
